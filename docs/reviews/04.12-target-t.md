## 1. The statement of Target T

### 1.1 The source

Target T comes from Jingjun Han and Jihao Liu, "On termination of flips and exceptionally non-canonical singularities", arXiv:2209.13122 (v2 of 2025-01-06), Geom. Topol. 29 (2025) 399-441. The numbers below (Definition 3.8, Conjecture 1.9, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, Lemma 6.2) are checked against the arXiv v2 text and against the text of the journal PDF [V]. The record is `papers/HL25.yaml`.

The brief for this review gave the number arXiv:2003.13514. That number is a paper on supergroup gauge theories, not this one. The spec (04.4, 07.14) has the right number, 2209.13122.

### 1.2 The definitions

We work over $\mathbb{C}$. These are the definitions of Han-Liu, section 3 [V].

- A pair $(X, B)$ is a normal variety $X$ and an $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $B \ge 0$ with $K_X + B$ $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier. For a prime divisor $E$ over $X$, the log discrepancy is $a(E, X, B) = 1 - \operatorname{mult}_E B_Y$, where $K_Y + B_Y = f^*(K_X + B)$ on a model $f : Y \to X$ where $E$ is a divisor.
- For a point $x \in X$, $\operatorname{mld}(X \ni x, B)$ is the infimum of $a(E, X, B)$ over the $E$ whose center on $X$ is $\bar{x}$.
- The global mld $\operatorname{mld}(X, B)$ is the infimum of $a(E, X, B)$ over all exceptional prime divisors $E$ over $X$. Han-Liu use it in this sense in the introduction ("the global mld of any normal variety is always $\le 2$", p. 2), and Definition 3.8 compares it with the local mld. Definition 3.4 writes out only the local form, so this reading is ours, but the text leaves little room for another [V].
- **Definition 3.8(1).** $(X, B)$ is exceptionally non-canonical (enc) if $\operatorname{mld}(X, B) < 1$ and there is exactly one exceptional prime divisor $E$ over $X$ with $a(E, X, B) \le 1$.
- **Definition 3.8(2).** A germ $(X \ni x, B)$ is enc if $(X, B)$ is enc near $x$ and $\operatorname{mld}(X \ni x, B) = \operatorname{mld}(X, B)$.
- **Lemma 3.9.** An enc pair is klt.

For $\Gamma \subset [0, 1]$ and $d \ge 1$, Han-Liu set
$$\mathrm{eMLD}_d(\Gamma) = \{\operatorname{mld}(X, B) : (X, B) \text{ is enc}, \dim X = d, \operatorname{coeff}(B) \subseteq \Gamma\}.$$

**Conjecture 1.9** [V].
1. If $\Gamma$ satisfies the DCC, then $\mathrm{eMLD}_d(\Gamma)$ satisfies the ACC.
2. If $\Gamma$ is finite, then $\mathrm{eMLD}_d(\Gamma)$ is discrete away from $0$.
3. (2') If $\Gamma$ is finite, then $\mathrm{eMLD}_d(\Gamma)$ satisfies the ACC.

A set satisfies the ACC when it has no infinite strictly increasing sequence. So (2') is a statement about infinite families: a single pair can never violate it. Theorem 1.2 of Han-Liu: Conjecture 1.9(2') in dimension 4 implies the termination of lc flips in dimension 4 [V].

### 1.3 The toric case

A toric pair, as in Ambro 2006, is a pair $(X, B)$ with $X$ a toric variety and $B$ torus invariant, so $B = \sum_i b_i V(e_i)$ over the rays $e_i$ of the fan [V]. The log discrepancy function $\psi$ is linear on each cone with $\psi(e_i) = 1 - b_i$, and the toric divisor of a primitive lattice point $e$ of the support has log discrepancy $\psi(e)$.

**Target T** (O-R3-3, as 04.12 means it). For every finite $\Gamma \subset [0, 1]$, the set
$$\mathrm{eMLD}^{\mathrm{tor}}_4(\Gamma) = \{\operatorname{mld}(X, B) : (X, B) \text{ an enc toric pair}, \dim X = 4, \operatorname{coeff}(B) \subseteq \Gamma\}$$
satisfies the ACC.

This is the reading of 04.12, which calls the target "convex geometry over $\mathbb{Z}^4$". A second reading is possible: $X$ is analytically toric near the point, and $B$ is any divisor with coefficients in $\Gamma$, not torus invariant. Section 2.4 treats it.

## 2. Is the toric case known?

**Finding: yes. Target T, as 04.12 states it, follows at once from Ambro 2006.** The enc condition and the dimension 4 play no role. The argument is in 2.2. Han-Liu say as much: "the ACC conjecture for mlds is only known in full generality for surfaces, toric pairs [Amb06], and exceptional singularities" (p. 2 to 3) [V].

### 2.1 What Ambro and Borisov prove

Ambro, "The set of toric minimal log discrepancies", Cent. Eur. J. Math. 4 (2006) 358-370 [V, read from the journal PDF]. For a set $A \subseteq [0, 1]$ with $1 \in A$, $\mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_d(A)$ is the set of $\operatorname{mld}(\eta; X, B)$ where $(X, B)$ is a toric log variety whose log discrepancies in codimension one lie in $A$, and $\eta$ is a point of codimension $d$. Theorem 1.1:
1. an explicit description of $\mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_d(A)$ (Proposition 3.3), as the union over $2 \le s \le d$ of the values $\sum_i x_i a_i$;
2. if $A$ satisfies the ACC, so does $\mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_d(A)$;
3. the accumulation points, when $A$ has no nonzero accumulation point.

Ambro also shows (section 2) that the mld of a toric pair at the closed orbit of an affine toric variety is $\min \psi$ over the lattice points of the interior of the cone, so toric divisors compute it [V].

Borisov, "Minimal discrepancies of toric singularities", Manuscripta Math. 92 (1997) 33-45, proves the case $B = 0$. We have this only from Ambro's introduction, and we did not read Borisov's paper [U].

The brief and the letter of Q-05 (question 4) speak of "the toric LSC of Ambro". Ambro 2006 proves the ACC, not the LSC. The LSC conjecture is Ambro 1999. The toric LSC is easy: the mld at a point of an orbit is the mld of the orbit plus a constant, and it does not enter here.

### 2.2 The argument [A]

Let $\Gamma \subset [0, 1]$ be finite, and let $(X, B)$ be an enc toric pair of dimension 4 with $\operatorname{coeff}(B) \subseteq \Gamma$. Put $A = \{1 - b : b \in \Gamma\} \cup \{1\}$. Then $A$ is finite, so it satisfies the ACC.

1. Each exceptional prime divisor $E$ over $X$ has a center $\eta$ of codimension $c \ge 2$, and each divisor with center $\eta$ is exceptional. So $\operatorname{mld}(X, B) = \inf_\eta \operatorname{mld}(\eta; X, B)$ over the points $\eta$ of codimension at least 2.
2. By Ambro section 2, $\operatorname{mld}(\eta; X, B)$ depends only on the orbit $O(\tau)$ that contains $\eta$ and on the codimension of $\eta$, and it is smallest at the generic point of the orbit. The fan has finitely many cones. So the infimum is a minimum over finitely many values, each of the form $\operatorname{mld}(\eta; X, B)$ with $\eta$ of codimension $c \in \{2, 3, 4\}$.
3. Each of these values is in $\mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_c(A)$, and by Proposition 3.3 of Ambro, $\mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_c(A) \subseteq \mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_4(A)$ for $c \le 4$.
4. So $\mathrm{eMLD}^{\mathrm{tor}}_4(\Gamma) \subseteq \mathrm{Mld}^{\mathrm{tor}}_4(A)$, which satisfies the ACC by Theorem 1.1(2). A subset of an ACC set satisfies the ACC.

The same four steps prove the toric case of Conjecture 1.9(1) in every dimension: if $\Gamma$ satisfies the DCC, then $A$ satisfies the ACC. For $B = 0$, Borisov's theorem is enough.

### 2.3 Conjecture 1.9(2) for toric pairs [A]

The stronger Conjecture 1.9(2), discreteness away from $0$, also seems to follow in the toric case, from Ambro and from Lemma 6.2 of Han-Liu. We give the argument because it would remove the most natural replacement for Target T. A person must check it.

**Lemma 6.2 of Han-Liu** [V]. For $d \ge 1$ and $\epsilon > 0$ there is an integer $I(d, \epsilon)$ such that: if $r \ge 1$ and $v_1, \dots, v_d$ satisfy $\sum_i (1 + (m-1) v_i - \lceil m v_i \rceil) \ge \epsilon$ for every integer $m \in [2, r]$, then $r \le I$. As printed, the lemma takes $v_i \in [0, 1]$, but it fails for $v = 0$ (the sum is then $d$ for every $m$), and the proof needs $v_i \in (0, 1]$. The application below has $v_i \in (0, 1]$. The proof uses Kronecker's theorem, so $I(d, \epsilon)$ is not explicit.

**The argument.** Let $(X, B)$ be an enc toric pair of dimension $d$ with coefficients in a finite $\Gamma$ and $\mu = \operatorname{mld}(X, B) \in [\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$.
1. The pair is klt (Lemma 3.9), so every $a_i = 1 - b_i$ is in $(0, 1]$, and $A$ is a finite subset of $(0, 1]$.
2. $\mu = \psi(v)$ for a primitive lattice point $v$ in the relative interior of a cone of the fan of dimension at least 2, and $E_v$ is the unique exceptional divisor with $a \le 1$. By Carathéodory (as in Ambro's proof of Proposition 3.3), $v = \sum_{i=1}^s x_i e_i$ with $2 \le s \le d$, the $e_i$ linearly independent rays of that cone, and $x_i \in (0, 1]$ (if $x_i > 1$, then $v - e_i$ is a smaller point of the interior). Let $q$ be the order of $x$ in $(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^s$.
3. For $2 \le m \le q$, the point $u_m = \sum_i (1 + m x_i - \lceil m x_i \rceil) e_i$ is a lattice point in the relative interior of the cone of $e_1, \dots, e_s$, and $u_m \ne v$. Its divisor is exceptional, and $\psi(u_m) - \psi(v) = \sum_i (1 + (m-1) x_i - \lceil m x_i \rceil) a_i$.
4. Write $u_m = k w$ with $w$ primitive. If $w \ne v$, then $\psi(u_m) \ge \psi(w) > 1$ because the pair is enc, so the difference is more than $1 - \mu \ge \epsilon$. If $w = v$, then $k \ge 2$ and the difference is $(k - 1) \mu \ge \epsilon$.
5. A form of Lemma 6.2 with weights $a_i \in (0, 1]$ then gives $q \le I(d, \epsilon)$. The proof of Lemma 6.2 makes each term of the sum small on its own, so the weights do not change it. We checked this only on paper, here. For $B = 0$ all $a_i = 1$, and Lemma 6.2 applies as printed.
6. So $x \in \frac{1}{q} \mathbb{Z}^s$ with $q \le I$, and $\mu = \sum_i x_i a_i$ takes finitely many values. So $\mathrm{eMLD}^{\mathrm{tor}}_d(\Gamma) \cap [\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ is finite for every $\epsilon$.
7. Near $1$: an infinite set of values in $[1 - \epsilon, 1)$ for every $\epsilon$ would contain an increasing sequence, which 2.2 excludes. So $\mathrm{eMLD}^{\mathrm{tor}}_d(\Gamma) \cap [\epsilon, 1)$ is finite for every $\epsilon > 0$, which is Conjecture 1.9(2) for toric pairs.

This is Han-Liu's own proof of their Theorem 6.3 (enc cyclic quotient 3-folds), done for any toric pair. We did not find it written for toric pairs in any dimension, but it is short, and an expert may know it [U].

### 2.4 Where a gap remains

The toric case of Conjecture 1.9 leaves nothing open in the reading of 1.3. A gap remains only in other readings, or in the other conjecture of Han-Liu.
1. **Toric $X$, any $B$.** If $X$ is a toric germ and $B$ is not torus invariant, the mld is not $\min \psi$, and Ambro does not apply. Already for $X = \mathbb{C}^4$ this is the ACC for mlds of pairs on a smooth 4-fold with coefficients in a finite set, restricted to enc pairs. We do not know a proof [U]. This reading is not convex geometry over $\mathbb{Z}^4$, it does not use the infrastructure of R3, and it is close to O-R4-7 (a fixed 4-fold) and to the claims of Chen-Lin 2609.20515, which we did not read [U].
2. **Conjecture 1.8 for toric enc 4-folds.** Conjecture 1.8 of Han-Liu [V]: for $d$, $\epsilon > 0$ and a DCC set $\Gamma$, there is $\delta > 0$ such that every enc germ $(X \ni x, B)$ of dimension $d$ with coefficients in $\Gamma$ (1) admits a $\delta$-plt blow-up, and (2) has local volume bounded away from $0$ when its mld is at least $\epsilon$. By Han-Liu-Shokurov (cited as [HLS24, Theorem 1.3]), Conjecture 1.8 implies Conjecture 1.9 [U]. For toric germs with torus invariant $B$ both parts are statements of convex geometry: the plt blow-up of the minimizer $v$ is the star subdivision at $v$, and the local volume of a toric germ is a minimum over the interior of the cone. Section 2.3 bounds the order of the minimizer, not the order of the group $N / \sum \mathbb{Z} e_i$, so it does not bound the local volume. We do not know whether the toric case of Conjecture 1.8 is known [U].
3. **Hyperquotient germs.** O-R3-4 (enc 4-fold hyperquotient germs, after Nakamura-Shibata 2606.24592, not read [U]) is not covered by Ambro.

### 2.5 What this means for the spec

O-R3-3 is a corollary of O-R3-2 (toric ACC for mlds, rated 1, known). Its rating "2 [U] (open)" in 04.4 is wrong. It fails the first criterion of 04.12: it is not open. It is still a good Lean deliverable: a Lean proof of Ambro's Theorem 1.1(2) on the toric code of R3 gives the Lean statement of Target T as a corollary. But that is a known result, not level G5.

## 3. What the E2 data says

The run `lab/runs/2026-11-27-e2-simplicial-mld-4-65e39813` lists the 81 simplicial toric 4-fold germs $\mathbb{C}^4/G$ with $|G| \le 6$ and $B = 0$.

1. **The counts.** 13 germs are enc in the sense of Definition 3.8(1) (the column `enc_hl`). The column `enc` (29 germs) is the older count without $\operatorname{mld} < 1$; it is not the definition of Han-Liu, and it should not be used for Target T.
2. **The values.** The global mlds of the 13 germs take 8 values: $1/3, 2/5, 1/2, 3/5, 2/3, 3/4, 4/5, 5/6$. Each has denominator at most 6, as it must: the minimizer is a point of $\frac{1}{|G|}\mathbb{Z}^4$.
3. **No candidate counterexample, and none is possible.** A finite list cannot violate an ACC statement, and by 2.2 no infinite family of toric pairs violates it either. Spec 13.5 says that "a violating germ for Target T would be a proved counterexample". No single germ can violate Target T, so this sentence should change.
4. **Global mld and the fixed point.** Only 3 of the 13 germs have their unique divisor over the fixed point: $\frac{1}{5}(1,1,1,1)$ (mld $4/5$), $\frac{1}{6}(1,1,1,1)$ (mld $2/3$) and $\frac{1}{6}(2,1,1,1)$ (mld $5/6$). These are the enc germs at the fixed point in the sense of Definition 3.8(2). In the other 10, the divisor lies over a curve or a surface, and the mld at the fixed point is at least $4/3$; for example $\frac{1}{3}(0,0,1,1)$ has global mld $2/3$ and mld $8/3$ at the fixed point. The E2 row of spec 13 says that the global mld of an affine toric enc 4-fold is the mld at the fixed point. This is false for these 10 germs. The run itself uses the global mld (`germ_mld`), which is the one Conjecture 1.9 needs.
5. **A pattern.** In the family $\frac{1}{r}(1,1,1,1)$ the box points have ages $4k/r$, so the germ is enc exactly for $r = 5, 6, 7$; $r = 7$ is beyond the run. This is the bounded order of 2.3 in the simplest family.
6. **What the run does not cover.** It has $B = 0$ only, while Target T has torus invariant boundaries with coefficients in a finite $\Gamma$. It has simplicial cones only. It counts toric divisors only. For a klt toric pair this is enough: a non-toric divisorial valuation has log discrepancy at least $1$ more than its monomial retraction on a toric log resolution, so it has $a > 1$ [U, a standard fact of the Jonsson-Mustață kind that we did not check in a source].

**The index-12 plan (Q-22).** Index 12 takes 0.03 s and gives 764 germs. It would add more of the same: more enc germs and more values, with the same checks. It cannot bear on the ACC, which is proved. If E2 is to serve the new target, it should measure other things:
- the order $q$ of the minimizer against the mld, to see the bound $I(4, \epsilon)$ of 2.3. An informal check outside the lab (cyclic germs only, $|G| \le 30$, not a record) found enc germs with mld in $[1/3, 2/3]$ whose minimizer has order 30, for example $\frac{1}{30}(23, 25, 26, 27)$ with mld $19/30$. So the bound is not reached below order 30, and index 12 will not show it;
- torus invariant boundaries with coefficients in a small finite $\Gamma$;
- the local volume and the plt blow-up of each enc germ, for Conjecture 1.8 (2.4);
- the germs that are not simplicial.

For these questions the relevant number is the order of the minimizer, not $|G|$ and not the Gorenstein index. This bears on the open part of Q-22.

## 4. The backups

The four criteria of 04.12 are: open, small enough for one paper, checkable in Lean, and on a route that the project builds anyway.

### 4.1 O-R6-2: Ajit's questions (E4)

Rahul Ajit, "Counterexamples to the Ambro-Kawamata effective non-vanishing conjecture", arXiv:2610.09270 v2, section 5.6, ends with three questions [V]:
1. Can a klt Fano variety over $\mathbb{C}$ carry a nef Cartier divisor $D$ with $H^0(X, D) = 0$? Ajit notes that the answer is no for smooth Fano manifolds of dimension at most 4, and that for $D = -K_X$ Cartier a counterexample needs dimension at least 6 (citing [CJ20], [HS25], [HS20], which we did not read [U]).
2. Given $N \ge 3$, is there a projective klt pair $(X, B)$ and a nef Cartier $D$ with $D - (K_X + B)$ nef and big and $H^0(X, kD) = 0$ for $1 \le k < N$? By Kollár's effective base point free theorem, long gaps can occur only in large dimension.
3. Is there a terminal projective $X$ with an ample Cartier $D$, $D - K_X$ ample, $H^0(X, D) = 0$, and $R(X, D)$ with rational singularities?

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| open | yes, as of October 2026, as the author poses them; we did not search for later answers [U] |
| one paper | yes for one example that answers (1) or (3); a general negative answer would be larger |
| checkable in Lean | an example is checkable `proved-mod` named hypotheses, as O-R1-1 is: Cartier, ample, $h^0$ by a count of monomials, terminal by Reid-Tai. "$R(X, D)$ has rational singularities" in (3) is far from Mathlib and would be a named hypothesis |
| on a built route | partly. R6 itself has no built infrastructure, but an example sits next to O-R1-1 (the check of Ajit's 4-fold, the first claim of Q-15) and uses the cyclic quotient code of E2, and weighted projective spaces are toric (R3) |

Two corrections to E4 (spec 13). First, E4 asks for "ample $D$" and must ask for ample **Cartier** $D$: with a $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier Weil divisor, examples with $H^0(D) = 0$ are known (Iano-Fletcher, Example 16.1, and Xie, as Ajit cites them [U]). Second, E4 searches dimension 3 for question (3) without the condition on $R(X, D)$, which is then a search for a counterexample to effective non-vanishing for terminal 3-folds. We did not check what is known in dimension 3 [U].

### 4.2 O-R8-1: a surface question on foliations

Spec 04.9 defines O-R8-1 as "do the notions of foliation singularity agree on normal surfaces? (Q2.3)", from Cascini and Spicer, "Recent progress on the Minimal Model Program for foliations", arXiv:2604.09027 v1. The question is more precise than this [V]. For a foliation given by a derivation $\partial$ on a normal variety $X$, there are two singular loci: $\operatorname{Sing} \partial = \{x : \partial(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}) \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x\}$, and $\operatorname{Sing} \mathcal{F}$, the cosupport of the image of the twisted Pfaff field $(\Omega^{[r]}_X \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-K_\mathcal{F}))^{**} \to \mathcal{O}_X$. Question 2.3 asks whether they agree on normal varieties. The survey says that the question is open even for surfaces, and that it holds when $X$ is klt (citing [CS25d, Proposition 2.32], which we did not read [U]). So the open case is a non-klt normal surface singularity, such as a cone over an elliptic curve. The text of O-R8-1 in 04.9 should say "the two notions of the singular locus".

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| open | yes, per the survey of April 2026 [U for later work] |
| one paper | yes: one local example or one local proof is a short note |
| checkable in Lean | the local question is commutative algebra over a 2-dimensional normal local ring (derivations, duals, exterior powers, which Mathlib has), so the statement is within reach. A counterexample needs a reflexive hull computed in Lean, which is not a finite arithmetic check |
| on a built route | no. R8 has weight 0.05 and no built parts, and the question uses no fans, no mlds and no lab code |

O-R8-1 is the weaker backup. It fails the fourth criterion.

## 5. Recommendation

**Change Target T. Do not swap in a backup yet.**

Reasons:
1. Target T as stated is not open. It follows at once from Ambro 2006 (2.2), and Han-Liu themselves list toric pairs as a known case. The stronger toric Conjecture 1.9(2) seems to follow from Ambro and Lemma 6.2 of Han-Liu (2.3). A G5 target must be open.
2. The work of R3 on Target T is not lost. Move O-R3-3 into O-R3-2: a Lean proof of Ambro's theorem gives the Lean statement `HanLiuEncACC` restricted to toric pairs (S52) as a corollary, and the E2 certificates of 13.5 still serve it.
3. The backups are weaker than the best replacement on the fourth criterion. O-R6-2 is a good first backup, after the fix of E4. O-R8-1 is not on a built route.

**The proposed new Target T**, to be confirmed by an expert:
1. **First choice: Conjecture 1.8 of Han-Liu for enc toric 4-fold germs with torus invariant boundary**, both parts: a uniform $\delta$-plt blow-up, and a lower bound for the local volume when the mld is at least $\epsilon$. It is convex geometry over $\mathbb{Z}^4$, on R3, the lab can compute both quantities, and by [HLS24] it is the hypothesis that implies Conjecture 1.9. Its status is not known to us [U], so it is open only if an expert says so.
2. **Second choice: O-R3-4**, Conjecture 1.9(2') for enc hyperquotient 4-fold germs. It is not covered by Ambro, and it is on R3 through Newton polyhedra, but it is harder (rated 3 [U]).
3. **Backups:** O-R6-2 first, O-R8-1 second.

Q-16 stays open. It is decided only when the owner and one expert, of the Han-Liu group or of the toric group, have read this review. Question 4 of 16.1 and of the letter of Q-05 can now be asked more sharply: "The toric case of Conjecture 1.9 follows from Ambro 2006 (and 1.9(2) from your Lemma 6.2). Is the toric case of Conjecture 1.8 known?"
