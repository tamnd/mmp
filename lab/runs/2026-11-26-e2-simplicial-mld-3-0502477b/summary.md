# 2026-11-26-e2-simplicial-mld-3-0502477b

For each simplicial toric 3-fold germ C^3/G, with G = N/Z^3 abelian of order r <= 1200 and the rays primitive, what are the mld, the terminal and canonical germs and the enc germs, is every terminal germ cyclic of type 1/r(1, -1, a), and do the isolated cyclic germs agree with the run e2-cyclic-quotient-mld-3 to index 40?

Result: agrees: in 110004193 simplicial toric 3-fold germs with r <= 1200, each terminal germ is cyclic of type 1/r(1, -1, a) with the mld 1 + 1/r, and the cyclic isolated germs with r <= 40 are those of the family cyclic.

## What the run does

The run lists each simplicial toric 3-fold germ with 2 <= r <= 1200, once up to isomorphism. Such a germ is the cone spanned by e_1, e_2, e_3 in a lattice N with Z^3 ⊆ N ⊂ Q^3, so it is a finite subgroup G = N/Z^3 of (Q/Z)^3, and r is the order of G, the index of Z^3 in N. It is not the Gorenstein index. The e_i are the primitive generators of the rays exactly when no nonzero element of G is on one coordinate, and two germs are isomorphic exactly when their groups differ by a permutation of the coordinates. The run lists the lattices N by the Hermite normal forms of their duals, which are the sublattices of Z^3 of index r, keeps those with primitive rays, and keeps the smallest normal form of the six permutations of the coordinates.

An element g of G is written (g_1, g_2, g_3) with 0 <= g_i < r, for g_i / r, and its age is (g_1 + g_2 + g_3) / r. The log discrepancy of the divisor of a primitive vector v of the cone is v_1 + v_2 + v_3. A lattice point of the interior of the cone is g plus an integer vector m >= 0 that is positive at the zero coordinates of g, so the mld at the torus-fixed point is the minimum of 3 and of age(g) + #{i : g_i = 0} over the nonzero g, and the minimizers are the points g + (the zero coordinates of g). The mld of the germ, over all exceptional divisors, also counts the divisors over the curves and is the minimum of 2 and of the ages of the nonzero g. The germ is terminal when each nonzero age is more than 1 and canonical when each is at least 1 (Reid-Tai). For an isolated germ the two mlds agree. For a germ that is not isolated they do not: 1/2(1,1,0), A1 times a line, has the mld 2 at the fixed point and the mld 1 as a germ, and it is canonical, not terminal.

The divisors of log discrepancy at most 1 are the primitive points g of the box with age(g) <= 1: a point g + m with m != 0 has log discrepancy more than 1. The count enc is the old one of E2: exactly one exceptional divisor of log discrepancy at most 1. Han-Liu, Definition 3.8(1), as the note of MMXCD16PTN reads it, also asks that the mld be less than 1, with the mld over all exceptional divisors. The count enc_hl adds that condition. The two differ on the germs whose one divisor has log discrepancy exactly 1, such as 1/2(1,1,0).

## The checks

- Terminal germs. A terminal simplicial toric 3-fold germ is cyclic of type 1/r(1, -1, a) with gcd(a, r) = 1 (White-Frumkin, Morrison-Stevens; the terminal lemma), and its mld is 1 + 1/r. Of the 218893 terminal germs, 0 are not cyclic, isolated, of this type and of this mld.
- The terminal lemma on the cyclic isolated germs, both ways: 0 disagree.
- An isolated germ of dimension 3 has a cyclic group: a group (Z/p)^2 in (Z/p)^3 meets each coordinate plane. 0 isolated germs are not cyclic. So no germ with a group that is not cyclic is terminal.
- The family cyclic. The 1747 cyclic isolated germs with r <= 40 are compared with a run of the family cyclic, germ for germ: the weights, the mld, a minimizer, terminal, canonical and the terminal lemma. 0 differ.
- The family cyclic counts at_most_one as the points of the box of age at most 1, and some of them are not primitive, so they are not divisors. 733 of the 1747 germs have a smaller count here; for example, 1/6[1 1 1] has 2 points of the box of age at most 1, and 1 primitive points. For 63 of them enc changes. This run counts the primitive points only; the family cyclic keeps its old count.

## Notes

E2 in dimension 3 for milestone S2, over all simplicial toric germs, cyclic or not, isolated or not, up to the permutations of the coordinates. The germs are enumerated by the Hermite normal form of the dual lattice. Terminal and canonical use the mld of the germ, the least age over G, so 1/2(1,1,0) is canonical and not terminal. enc counts the primitive box points of age at most 1; enc_hl adds mld < 1 (Han-Liu Definition 3.8(1)). germs.json lists the germs of index at most 30; indices.json counts every index. The work grows like r^4. Measured on a loaded 10-core arm64 laptop, index 600 took 4.5 min, index 800 7 min and index 1000 12.4 min, so index 1200, at about 26 min, is the largest that runs in about 30 min. It has no Lean certificate yet (13.4).

## What the run does not do

- It is a search, not a proof. Only a certificate that Lean checks makes a theorem (spec 13.4).
- It covers the simplicial germs of dimension 3 only, not the germs that are not simplicial and not dimension 4.
- It counts the toric divisors. That the toric divisors compute the mld of a toric germ is a theorem of route R3 (spec 13.5), not a part of the run.
- It runs in the mmp binary, not in a container with pinned images, and it uses no network and no other tool. The record gives the commit of mmp, so the outputs are reproducible.
